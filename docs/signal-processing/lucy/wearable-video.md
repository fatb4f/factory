# Wearable video

Use a dedicated chest-mounted body camera as the first hardware candidate for **courier-initiated incident capture** and optional live viewing by dispatch. Test framing with the coat and bag Clara actually wears. Smart glasses give a head-level view, but head movement can take her hands and the package out of frame. A phone may serve as both beacon and camera, or the two functions can use separate devices.

| Candidate | Documented capability | Procurement or test question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Motorola V500 | Pre-recording, LTE, and an indicator when a VideoManager operator views the stream | Confirm local capture during an outage, streaming license, Canadian sales terms, and restricted viewer access |
| Axon Body 4 | Configurable pre-event buffer of up to 120 seconds | Confirm the full kit, subscription and Canadian availability for a small courier operator |
| Ray-Ban Meta Gen 2 | Individual video clips of up to three minutes | Test whether head-mounted framing is useful for specific tasks |

For a staged trial, use volunteers and dummy cargo, keep audio off, restrict viewing to the assigned dispatcher, and show the courier when the device is capturing or streaming. When aerial visibility drops, activate the wearable and interrupt cellular service. Check one-press activation, local clip survival, how dispatch sees a stale feed, time to recover a usable view, and whether the exchange appears in frame. Keep any test media on a documented retention schedule. Continuous recording or live monitoring across an entire delivery would require a separate scope decision and privacy assessment.
